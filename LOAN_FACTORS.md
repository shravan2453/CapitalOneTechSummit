# Comprehensive Loan Factors to Consider

This list covers inputs, product attributes, and risk/compliance points for optimizing student loan plans.

## Borrower/household inputs
- Student info: age, dependency status, year level, graduation date, program type, enrollment status.
- School/COA: cost of attendance per year, grants/scholarships, tuition growth assumption (e.g., 7%/yr).
- Residence: state of residence (impacts state aid).
- Income: current/projected income, growth rate, parent income (for Parent PLUS and expected family contribution), cosigner income/credit.
- Family size/dependents: for IDR discretionary income.
- Budget: monthly post-grad budget; tolerance for short/med/long-term payments.
- Credit profile: credit score band; delinquencies; cosigner yes/no.
- Goals: target payoff year; priority of min payment vs min total cost; risk appetite.
- In-school payment choice: defer, interest-only, fixed partial, full.

## Federal loans
- Direct Subsidized: annual/aggregate caps by dep status/year; interest subsidized in-school; fixed rate; origination fee; grace period; standard term 10y; eligible for IDR (SAVE/PAYE).
- Direct Unsubsidized: caps (higher for independent); interest accrues in-school; same rate/fee/grace; IDR eligible.
- Parent PLUS / Grad PLUS: up to remaining COA; higher rate/fee; credit check; not subsidized; can consolidate for IDR; deferment options.
- IDR overlays: discretionary income = (income – 225% poverty guideline * household); payment % and forgiveness term (20/25y); interest subsidy rules under SAVE; potential tax on forgiveness.
- Aggregate limits must not be exceeded; annual limits by academic year.

## State loans (varies by state; examples)
- NC Assist / FELS (NC): residency, program, rate range, term options, possible forgivability (FELS).
- CA CalEdge (example), NY HESC (example): residency, rate bands, term options, fees.
- Other state agencies: check residency, school eligibility, rate caps, fees, forgiveness/teacher/nursing-specific benefits.
- References: [Duke Loans overview](https://financialaid.duke.edu/types-aid/loans/) and [Recommended lender list](https://personalfinance.duke.edu/student-loans-101/recommended-lender-list/).

## Private loans
- APR by credit tier and cosigner; variable vs fixed; term options (5/10/15/20).
- In-school options: defer, interest-only, fixed partial, immediate repayment.
- Cosigner release terms; origination/prepayment fees (often none); approval criteria (DTI, income).
- Rate discounts: autopay, cosigner, relationship.

## Calculation/optimization considerations
- Tuition growth: compound COA each remaining year; recompute funding gap for multi-year planning.
- Fill order: subsidized → unsubsidized → state → private, honoring annual/aggregate caps.
- In-school accrual: deferment capitalizes interest (except subsidized benefit); interest-only/fixed payments partially offset accrual.
- Payment strategies: standard vs IDR; choose displayed payment as min(standard, IDR early years) but keep total cost metrics.
- Scoring: minimize total paid/NPV while respecting budget and target payoff; surface affordability warnings.
- Monte Carlo: income variability impacts IDR; rate sensitivity for private variable loans.

## Compliance/UX notes
- Show origination fees, total cost, and payoff estimates; highlight uncovered gap.
- Warn on affordability, rate variability, cosigner obligations, and forgiveness tax risk.
- Provide “not financial advice” disclaimer; encourage consultation for binding decisions.
